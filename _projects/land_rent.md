## Research Overview  

As part of the National Undergraduate Training Program for Innovation and Entrepreneurship, 
this project explored whether classical land rent theory remains valid in the context of 
China’s megacity regions.  

## Methods  

- Spatial syntax analysis to calculate accessibility indices  
- Correlation of accessibility with land values  
- Comparative regional analysis across interconnected cities  

## Findings  

- Central areas still showed higher land values consistent with rent theory  
- Peripheral interconnections created deviations from classical patterns  
- Regional accessibility played a strong role in shaping urban networks  

## Outputs  

- National Innovation Program project deliverable  
- Maps and models of spatial accessibility and land value  
- Contribution to discussions on urban economic geography
